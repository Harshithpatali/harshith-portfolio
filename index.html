Customer Intelligence & Graph Anomaly Lab

A customer investigation workspace built from behavioural profiles and entity relationships. It combines customer-level summaries, K-means segmentation, a cohort-relative behaviour index, Isolation Forest and Local Outlier Factor anomaly ranking, and a sampled user–device–IP graph. Analysts can move from a segment-level signal to a customer history and then inspect shared entities that may explain the connection.

Scope: eight dashboard workspaces cover overview, Customer 360, customer exploration, segmentation, graph intelligence, anomaly review, executive export and methodology. Purchase timelines retain up to 50 recent transactions per customer.

Analytical design: the anomaly score blends Isolation Forest percentile (60%) with LOF percentile (40%). A separate 0–100 behavioural risk index is heuristic and excluded from anomaly model inputs to avoid circularity.

Interpretation: the historical fraud class is dropped from the current unsupervised workflow. Risk and anomaly outputs are cohort-relative investigation signals—not calibrated fraud probabilities or evidence of wrongdoing. They should not be used alone to block or penalise customers.

04Forecasting & optimisation

Retail Demand & Inventory Optimisation

An end-to-end retail decision system that joins global demand forecasting with a periodic-review (R,S) inventory policy. It profiles intermittent SKU demand, compares forecasting baselines, estimates uncertainty from out-of-sample residuals and simulates the cost of stock, replenishment and shortages.

Five-way validation comparison: XGBoost WAPE 0.2808 vs 0.6873 for the naive forecast (59.2% lower). Held-out inventory simulation: total modeled cost −16.21%, stockout units −30.65%, service level 91.96% → 94.42% (+2.46 pp).
SCOPE

M5 Forecasting data: 30,490 candidate item–store series and ~65.7M historical units. The reproducible deployment subset selects 200 series and produces 388,200 feature rows; 68.2% of item–store–day observations have zero demand.

WHAT IT DEMONSTRATES

Shows how forecasting quality affects a concrete operating decision. The selected policy holds less average inventory and reduces stockouts, while ordering cost rises 124.75% because it replenishes more frequently in smaller quantities.

Limitations: Cost and service results depend on explicit demonstration assumptions (e.g. 7-day lead/review periods and configured holding, ordering and stockout costs); they are not calibrated savings for a named retailer.

06Signal processing & forensics

DeepFake Noise & Wavelet Forensics

An interpretable image-forensics study testing whether spatial noise statistics and multi-resolution wavelet coefficients capture traces left by synthetic or manipulated face imagery. A fixed grayscale preprocessing pipeline feeds feature selection and regularised logistic regression rather than an end-to-end CNN.

Validation ROC-AUC 0.9249, but the locked official test is materially lower: ROC-AUC 0.6623, PR-AUC 0.6675, accuracy 0.6232, recall 0.8758 and precision 0.6153. A separate 100-image blind holdout produced ROC-AUC 0.4872 and 56% accuracy.
SCOPE

DeepFakeFusion-304K: 304,154 labelled images (230,361 train / 48,289 validation / 25,504 official test). Each 256×256 image produces 105 handcrafted descriptors: spatial, high-frequency noise, gradient/Laplacian and db2 level-3 wavelet statistics.

WHAT IT DEMONSTRATES

The main contribution is an auditable comparison of spatial and multi-scale forensic features, with a measurable but modest wavelet/noise advantage on the working dataset. The test/holdout gap is a useful lesson in generalisation and source shift.

Limitations: The strong validation numbers do not transfer to the locked test and small blind holdout. It should be treated as a research prototype; it does not establish universal deepfake detection or forensic-grade reliability.

Python · PyWavelets · scikit-learn · Logistic Regression

14Wavelet signal processing

Wavelet Voice Lab

A wavelet-domain voice-communication simulator: discrete wavelet decomposition, coefficient thresholding, keyed permutation, noisy-channel transport and receiver-side reconstruction. It includes a browser-based transmitter/receiver path and a headless experiment runner.

The verification dashboard reports recovered SNR, MSE, correlation, retained coefficients and packet recovery. Unit tests cover codec round-trip, channel behaviour and permutation reversibility; no aggregate speech-quality benchmark is published.
SCOPE

Wavelet controls cover Haar, db2–db8, sym4 and coif1 with decomposition levels 1–6. The research lab sweeps up to 180 parameter combinations across SNR, packet loss, threshold and wavelet; it supports AWGN and burst-loss channels, CRC32 packet checks and one-packet-per-four XOR recovery.

03 / Research interest

Wavelets × attention.

A direction I want to investigate at the intersection of signal processing, representation learning and Transformer architectures.

CURRENT QUESTION / NOT A COMPLETED METHOD

Can multi-resolution structure make attention more selective?

I’m interested in wavelet-based attention mechanisms for Transformers: using wavelet decompositions to separate coarse structure from fine-scale detail, then studying how attention should combine information across those scales.

The idea connects naturally to time series and audio, where useful patterns can appear at different temporal resolutions. I want to test whether scale-aware representations improve efficiency or robustness without sacrificing important local information.

Research guardrailThis is an exploratory interest, not a claim that I have already developed or benchmarked a new attention architecture.

Concept sketch / multi-resolution sequence

Questions: How should scale-specific tokens interact? Can attention be restricted to meaningful bands? What is the compute/accuracy trade-off against standard attention?

04 / Working principles

How I approach a problem.

I try to make technical work easier to trust: define the decision, understand the data, establish a baseline and keep the evaluation separate from the story I want the model to tell.

01 / FRAME

Start with the decision

Define the unit of analysis, target, time horizon and action the result should inform.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Respect the data

Check grain, missingness, leakage, sampling and the process that created the observations.

03 / COMPARE

Earn model complexity

Build simple baselines and use time-aware or held-out evaluation where the problem requires it.

04 / COMMUNICATE

Explain the limits

Separate measured outcomes from assumptions, projections and ideas that still need testing.
